# MARROW
### A Sword & Sorcery Roleplaying Game

# DESIGN NOTES

*For the Warden. This section is the only place the game's ancestry is discussed. Past the line below, the book stands alone.*

MARROW is built on the chassis of Mothership, a science-fiction horror game by Tuesday Knight Games. The character math is carried over intact: Stats at 2d10+25, Saves at 2d10+10, Health at 1d10+10, Stress beginning at 2, Panic rolled on 1d20 against current Stress, and the Wounds, Death, and Cover tables essentially unchanged. Any creature or scenario written for that system can be run here with a vocabulary swap and little else.

Four things are genuinely different.

**The Blight is the engine.** In the parent game the ticking resource is oxygen, which refills at the airlock. Blight does not refill. It is permanent, it only accumulates, and it is also the sole source of supernatural power in the setting. A party under pressure is a party deciding how much of itself to spend, and the decision cannot be unmade.

**Sorcery is player-facing, but only through contamination.** Anyone carrying Blight may attempt a Working. Effect scales with Blight Level. Reliability scales down with it, because Blight subtracts from every Stat and Save including the Sanity Save that governs control. Using it makes you stronger and less able to hold it. At Blight 10 you have 1d5 days. There is no plateau and no build, only a slope.

**Weapons beat armor types.** Crushing beats Plate, Piercing beats Mail, Cutting beats Soft, and each is poor against one other. It resolves as a single Advantage or Disadvantage on the Combat Check, and a weapon's Wound column already states which kind it is, so nothing new needs looking up.

**The economy is anchored on a day's wage.** One silver penny is roughly half a day of unskilled labor. Every price, wage, treatment cost, and Shore Leave fee derives from that figure rather than from the parent game's credit values, which do not survive the transfer.

# MARROW

> *Ambition is the shovel you dig your own grave with.*

## THE WORLD, IN BRIEF

There was an age of wonders. It ended the way such ages end: badly, and in a manner nobody living can explain.

What it left behind is the **Blight**. It seeps out of barrows and drowned temples, out of the floors of rooms where something was worked, out of relics that should have been left under six feet of good clean earth. It is not a spirit and it wants nothing. It is a stain, it spreads, and the closer you stand the more of it goes into you.

Everyone knows this much. Almost nobody knows what to do about it. So a village that finds bad ground rings it in stones, lets it go to thorn and nettle, and drives the herd the long way round for a generation, and tells the children the story often enough that they will tell it to theirs.

That is the whole of human policy on the matter, and it is not working. The bad ground spreads a little each year. There is more of it than there are stones.

So the great houses, the merchant guilds, and the chapters hire people to go in and bring things out. Relics for study. Relics for sale. Relics to be buried somewhere else, which is what most of them mean by study.

You are one of those people. You are not a hero. You are labor with a weapon, working under a contract you could not read, for a house whose steward has already calculated what you are worth dead and found the figure smaller than you would like.

Go in. Take what is worth taking. Come back out, and know that most do not.

## BEFORE YOU PLAY

This game deals in fear, injury, sickness, and dying badly, because that is the story it is built to tell. It works best when everyone at the table has arrived wanting that story and has a rough idea of the shape it will take.

So talk about it first. Say what you are hoping for, what you want the game to dwell on, and where your own limits sit. That conversation costs ten minutes, and it is the thing that lets a table commit fully to the grim parts later rather than flinching through them. Keep checking in as you go, because people find their limits in play rather than before it.

A table that has agreed on the experience can go considerably darker, and enjoy it far more, than one that has not.

## WHAT TO EXPECT

- **Characters die.** Play sharp. Make the time you have count for something.
- **Violence is punishing.** If you are fighting, you have already lost ground. Avoid it at nearly any price.
- **The odds are against you.** They are meant to be. Your task is to stack them, not to beat them straight.
- **The choices are bad.** Surviving, solving the thing, and saving anyone are frequently three different outcomes, and you may only get one.
- **Ask questions.** Information is the only armor that never breaks. Every question you ask is a roll you do not have to make, and every roll is a chance to lose something.

## WHAT YOU NEED

The book. A character sheet. **Four or more ten-sided dice** and **one twenty-sided die**: you will roll 1d100 as a pair of d10s for Checks and Saves, up to 4d10 at once for damage, and the d20 for Panic and nothing else. Pen and paper. Something to eat.

# I. MAKING YOUR CHARACTER

Nine steps. Follow them in order and do not agonize over the numbers.

A character here is made interesting by what happens to them, not by what they rolled at the start, and what happens to them is largely out of your hands. Nobody at this table will remember your Strength. They will remember the one who went in with a rusted sword and came back out with something else. Roll fast and start playing.

**1. Roll your Stats.** Roll 2d10 and add 25. Do this four times, once each for **Strength**, **Speed**, **Intellect**, and **Combat**. Around 36 is an ordinary person.

**2. Roll your Saves.** Roll 2d10 and add 10. Do this three times, once each for **Sanity**, **Fear**, and **Body**.

**3. Choose your Class.** Soldier, Blighted, Scholar, or Laborer. Apply its adjustments.

**4. Roll your Health.** Roll 1d10 and add 10. That is your Maximum Health. You begin at full Health with zero Wounds.

**5. Take your Stress.** Current Stress and Minimum Stress both begin at **2**.

**6. Note your Trauma Response.** Every class breaks differently, and every class takes someone down with it.

**7. Take your Class Skills and choose your Bonus Skills.** To take an Expert Skill you must first hold one of its Trained prerequisites. To take a Master Skill you must first hold one of its Expert prerequisites.

**8. Roll your Loadout, your Trinket, and your Crest.** Then roll 2d10 and multiply by 10 for your starting silver.

**9. Name yourself.** Write a **Tally** of zero. It counts the jobs you have walked away from. It does nothing at all. Others have died for less.

# II. THE FOUR CLASSES

> *Every trade is a way of being killed. Choose the one that suits your temper.*

---

## THE SOLDIER

Sellswords, levy veterans, free company hands, gaol-guards, oathbreakers, and deserters wearing somebody else's coat. You are good in a fight, and it is a shame that you will most likely die in one.

You have seen what a body does when it comes apart and you have made peace with it, or made something that passes for peace at a distance. That holds until it does not. When a soldier finally breaks, everyone nearby remembers what that soldier has seen, and they break too.

**Adjustments:** +10 Combat, +10 Body Save, +20 Fear Save, +1 Maximum Wound.

**Skills:** *Soldiering*, *Athletics*. **Bonus:** one Expert Skill, or two Trained Skills.

**Trauma Response:** Whenever you Panic, every Close friendly character must make a Fear Save.

---

## THE BLIGHTED

You went somewhere you should not have gone, or you were born downwind of it, or somebody handed you a box and said *do not open this* and you were curious.

It is in you now, and it does not leave. It has burned out whatever part of a person flinches and put something else in the empty space. You think faster and colder than you used to. You understand things nobody taught you. The dark holds nothing for you, because you have already met what lives in it and it looked back.

Your hair comes out in your hand. Your gums bleed. Your shadow does not always agree with you. You are dying, plainly and on a schedule, and everyone who travels with you can watch it happening.

**Adjustments:** +20 Intellect, -10 to one Stat of your choice, +60 Fear Save, +1 Maximum Wound.

**Blight:** You begin play at **Blight 3**. The penalties for those first three levels are already accounted for in the adjustments above. Every level after the third bites for real. See *The Blight*.

**The Blight sustains you.** You do not hunger, thirst, or sleep, and you ignore Exposure and Foul Air. None of this is comfortable.

**Skills:** *Mysticism*, *Linguistics*, and one Trained Skill from the life you had before this happened. **Bonus:** one Expert Skill, or two Trained Skills.

**Trauma Response:** Fear Saves made by Close friendly characters are at Disadvantage.

---

## THE SCHOLAR

Physicians, anatomists, alchemists, antiquarians, cartographers, torturers, and clerks of the black books. Anyone whose first response to a sealed thing is to want it open.

Understand what you are. You are not merely clever. You are one of perhaps four hundred people in the world who can read, and among those four hundred you are one of a handful who has read anything worth reading. The people you travel with cannot write their own names. They believe sickness comes from foul air and moral failure. They think the heart does the thinking. When they meet something they cannot explain they will look at you, and whatever you say next becomes the truth for everyone in the room.

That is the closest thing to power you will ever hold. It is also why they will never entirely trust you, and why some of them will quietly despise you for it. Knowledge in this world belongs to the houses, the guilds, and the temples. You have it. They do not. Everybody in the room can feel the shape of that.

**Adjustments:** +10 Intellect, +5 to one Stat of your choice, +30 Sanity Save.

**Skills:** *Letters*, *Mathematics*, and one Master Skill together with one Expert and one Trained Skill drawn from its chain of prerequisites.

**Trauma Response:** Whenever you fail a Sanity Save, all Close friendly characters gain 1 Stress.

---

## THE LABORER

Miners, teamsters, masons, sailors, porters, ratcatchers, ropemen, and everyone else who has been paid by the day since they were nine years old. Some companies call them hands. Most call them worse.

You know how the winch works. You know which prop is about to go, how much a wet rope will hold, and what it means when a ceiling starts to weep. Nobody has ever asked you a question and then written the answer down. You have buried more people than the priest has.

You are not special. You are simply harder to kill than the people who employ you, and you have been at this long enough that very little surprises you anymore.

**Adjustments:** +5 to all Stats, +10 to all Saves.

**Skills:** *Heavy Labor*, *Ropework*. **Bonus:** one Trained Skill and one Expert Skill.

**Trauma Response:** Once per session, you may take Advantage on a Panic Check.

# III. SKILLS

> *You know your trade, and the road to the next village. Everything past that is rumor.*

A Skill is a body of training you actually possess. When you make a Stat Check or a Save and a Skill genuinely applies, add its bonus to the Stat or Save before you roll, giving you a higher number to roll under.

- **Trained: +10.** A completed apprenticeship, or a few hard years on the job.
- **Expert: +15.** A mastership, or a working lifetime.
- **Master: +20.** You are one of a very small number of people alive who knows this.

## III.1 WHAT YOU DO NOT KNOW

**Most things can be attempted without the Skill.** You climb, you swim, you haggle, you track a deer badly, you set a broken finger, you guess at what a carving means. You get no bonus, and if the task is genuinely difficult you roll at Disadvantage, but you roll.

There is a short list of exceptions. These are not hard. **They are impossible**, and no roll is offered.

- **Reading or writing without *Letters*.** Not slowly, not badly, not at Disadvantage. Written words are shapes. A contract, a warning above a door, a name on a tomb, a label on a bottle: somebody has to tell you what it says, and you have no way at all to know whether they lied. You make your mark, and your mark can be forged by anyone with a steady hand.
- **Speaking or understanding a language you have never heard without *Linguistics*.**
- **The subject of any Master Skill.** Surgery, the design of a bridge that will stand, the naming of a thing that should not exist. You cannot approximate these. You would not know where to begin.
- **Operating a mechanism you have never seen before** without the relevant Skill: a lock, a mill's gearing, a siege engine, a sealed door with a mechanism behind it.
- **Formal medicine without *Field Medicine* or *Surgery*.** You may bind a wound, apply pressure, and hold somebody down. You may not set a compound fracture, take a limb, or say what is wrong with them.

**Reasoning it out.** If a character could plausibly work something out from what is in front of them, using ordinary sense rather than training, the Warden may allow an **Intellect Check at Disadvantage** in place of a refusal. A miner who has never studied architecture can still tell that an arch is about to fail. This never applies to literacy. You cannot reason your way into reading.

**What you believe instead.** Sickness comes from foul air, bad water, corrupted humors, and sin, roughly in that order. Comets announce the death of kings. A drowned man's hand cures warts. Iron turns away the fair folk. A child born under the wrong moon should be watched carefully. Most of these are wrong. At least one is not, and nobody has told you which.

## III.5 LEARNING A SKILL

Time is the real price. Silver is only the part you can borrow.

- **Trained:** 2 years and 500 silver in fees, materials, and guild dues.
- **Expert:** 4 years and 2,500 silver. Requires one Trained prerequisite.
- **Master:** 6 years and 10,000 silver. Requires one Expert prerequisite.

These figures assume you are also working, travelling, and staying alive. Full-time study under a house, a monastery, or a proper master halves the time and roughly doubles what it costs you in ways that are not silver.

While a character trains they are not working. Set them aside and play somebody else until the years run out. They may not be there when you get back.

## III.6 A TERM OF SERVICE

*Soldiering* is the one Skill that is free, because the price is paid in a different currency.

You sign for six years with a free company, a lord's household, or the levy. Set your character aside and play another until the term ends. When it does, roll a **Combat Check** to find out what six years did to you.

- **Success.** You gain *Soldiering*, *Athletics*, and two Trained Skills. Gain +10 Combat and reduce another Stat by 10. Gain the Soldier's Trauma Response.
- **Critical Success.** As a Success, but take one Expert Skill instead of the two Trained Skills. You came back with something.
- **Failure.** You gain *Soldiering*, *Athletics*, and one Trained Skill, and the Soldier's Trauma Response. You came back.
- **Critical Failure.** You did not come back.

# IV. SILVER

Everything is counted in **silver pennies**. Coins are clipped, shaved, foreign, and frequently false, and nobody at your level ever sees a fresh one.

**One penny is about half a day of unskilled work.** Everything below follows from that.

| | |
|---|---|
| A day of unskilled labor | 2 sp |
| A day of skilled labor: mason, carpenter, smith | 5 sp |
| A day of soldiering under contract | 8 sp |
| A day's bread, cheese, and small beer | 1 sp |
| A night on the common floor of a bad inn | 1 sp |
| A private room, a meal, and a bath | 5 sp |
| A decent burial, with a priest | 20 sp |
| A month's rent on a room in a free town | 30 sp |
| A mule | 100 sp |
| A riding horse | 800 sp |
| A mail hauberk | 1,200 sp |
| A working farm with its land | 5,000 sp |
| A knight's full harness | 6,000 sp |

A dangerous job pays a company somewhere between 200 and 1,500 silver a head, which is why anyone does it. A year of unskilled work pays about 600.

**Starting silver:** roll 2d10 and multiply by 10. That is what you have on top of your Loadout.

If you would rather buy your own gear and take nothing on trust, forgo the Loadout and roll **2d10 x 25** instead. You will be better armed and you will look like somebody with a reason to be, because you are.

# X. HOW TO PLAY

> *You may attempt anything. The dice decide only whether fortune was with you.*

One of you is the **Warden**. The Warden prepares a place, describes it, and answers questions honestly. The rest of you say what your characters do. Most of what you say simply happens.

**Roll only when failure would cost something.** Life, limb, time, armor, silver, or nerve. If failing costs nothing, you succeed and the game moves on. If failing would cost something but you have all the time in the world and nothing is coming, you succeed too; it merely takes a while. The dice come out when there is something at stake and something to lose.

## X.1 STAT CHECKS

When you want to do something and failing would hurt, roll **1d100 and try to roll under the relevant Stat.**

- **Strength:** holding a door shut against whatever is pushing on the far side. Carrying a bleeding companion out at a run. Climbing a wall with something climbing after you.
- **Speed:** getting through the gap before it closes. Moving before it does. Outrunning what is faster than you and has more legs.
- **Intellect:** remembering your training with a blade at your throat. Working a lock as the water comes up. Inventing an answer in the time you have, which is not enough time.
- **Combat:** fighting for your life, which is the only sort of fighting there is.

Roll under and you succeed. Otherwise you fail and **gain 1 Stress**. A roll of 90 to 99 always fails, however high your Stat.

## X.2 SAVES

When something is being done to you and you would like it to stop, roll **1d100 under the relevant Save.**

- **Sanity:** making sense of what cannot be, seeing through mimicry and false shapes, holding your understanding of the world together, bearing Stress.
- **Fear:** holding your nerve against terror, grief, isolation, and the surge that makes people run.
- **Body:** reflexes, cold, hunger, poison, disease, and things that want to get inside you.

Roll under and you succeed. Otherwise you fail and **gain 1 Stress**. 90 to 99 always fails.

## X.3 ADVANTAGE AND DISADVANTAGE

With a situational advantage, marked **[+]**, roll twice and take the better result. With a situational disadvantage, marked **[-]**, roll twice and take the worse. They cancel each other out.

## X.4 CRITICALS

Roll doubles on 1d100 and the result is a **Critical**. A successful critical is a **Critical Success** and something notably good happens. A failed critical is a **Critical Failure**: something bad happens, and **you must make a Panic Check.** A roll of 00 is always a Critical Success. A roll of 99 is always a Critical Failure.

## X.5 SKILLS

If you hold a Skill that genuinely applies, add its bonus before you roll, giving you a higher number to roll under. If you do not hold it, remember *What You Do Not Know*.

# XI. STRESS AND PANIC

> *A person is a rope. Everything that happens to them is a strand.*

**Stress** measures what the work has taken out of you. By itself it does nothing. It sits there and decides how likely you are to come apart when the moment arrives.

You gain **1 Stress every time you fail a Stat Check or a Save.** Some places and some things give it to you for merely being present. Your **Minimum Stress** begins at 2 and can be driven up permanently. **Maximum Stress is 20**; anything past 20 instead reduces the most relevant Stat or Save by that amount.

## XI.1 REST

You relieve Stress by resting somewhere reasonably safe. Make a **Rest Save using your worst Save**. On a success, reduce Stress by the ones digit of the roll: roll 24 under a worst Save of 30 and you shed 4 Stress. On a failure, gain 1 Stress instead.

Drink, prayer, company, a hot meal, poppy, or a night of ruinous gambling all grant Advantage on a Rest Save. Unsafe ground grants Disadvantage. A hard road grants nothing at all.

## XI.2 PANIC CHECKS

**Roll 1d20 and try to roll higher than your current Stress.** Roll equal or under and you fail: consult the Panic Table.

You must make a Panic Check whenever you roll a Critical Failure. The Warden may call for one at any other reasonable moment: watching a companion die, seeing more than one companion break at once, meeting something for the first time, or when hope has plainly run out. You may also call for one yourself, whenever you feel your character has earned it.

## XI.3 CONDITIONS

Some Panic results leave a **Condition**, which is permanent until treated. Conditions are not flavor. They follow you into every session that comes after, and treating them costs more silver than you have.

# XII. VIOLENCE

> *If you are fighting, something has already gone wrong. Everything after this is damage control.*

## XII.1 THE ROUND

A round is about ten seconds, and everything in it happens at once.

The Warden describes the situation and states plainly **what will happen if nobody does anything.** Then each of you says what your character does. Ask questions; the Warden answers honestly and names the likely cost of failing. Once everyone has committed, the Warden assigns whatever Checks and Saves are needed and everyone rolls together. Damage is rolled, the situation is described anew, and the next round begins.

**Strict order, if you want it:** everyone makes a Speed Check at the start. Those who succeed act before the enemy. Those who fail act after.

## XII.2 SURPRISE

If you might be ambushed, or simply stunned by what you are looking at, make a **Fear Save**. Succeed and you act. Fail and you stand there until next round.

## XII.3 WHAT YOU CAN DO

Move within Close Range, and do **one thing.**

Attack. Bandage a wound. Reload. Move again. Throw something to someone, or at them. Open, bar, or wedge a door. Work a winch, a bellows, a portcullis. Drag a body. Study the way a thing moves and look for the pattern in it. Talk, if you think talking will help.

**Quick things.** A few actions cost almost no time: drawing a weapon, dropping what you are holding, shouting a warning, kicking a lever that is already within reach. The Warden may let you do one of these and still take your action. If it genuinely split your attention or your footing, take the action at **[-]**.

If you do nothing at all except run, you may move within **Long Range**.

Your character sheet is a short document. It is not the list of what you may attempt. Distract so that somebody else can aim. Cut the rope. Collapse the floor. Take the lantern and leave. Outlast, outrun, and outthink. Nothing else has ever worked.

## XII.4 ATTACKING

Make a **Combat Check**, applying [+] or [-] from the kind of armor your target is wearing. On a success, roll the weapon's damage and subtract it from the enemy's Health. On a failure the situation gets worse and you gain 1 Stress.

## XII.5 ARMOR

You ignore all damage **less than** your Armor Points. If a single hit deals damage **equal to or greater than** your AP, the armor is destroyed and the remainder goes into you.

Armor Points from armor, shield, and cover all add together.

**Anti-Armor (AA)** ignores and destroys armor on any hit.

**Damage Reduction (DR)** always subtracts first, before armor is considered, and keeps working after the armor is gone and against Anti-Armor.

## XII.6 COVER

The world will protect you if you let it. Cover is destroyed exactly like armor.

| Cover | AP |
|---|---|
| **Insignificant:** a table on its side, a wooden door, a cart's panel | AP 5 |
| **Light:** a tree, a low wall, a stone pillar, a laden wagon | AP 10 |
| **Heavy:** a barred gate, a buttress, a fallen column, a ship's hull | DR 5, AP 20 |

**If you attack from cover, you are considered out of cover until your next turn.**

# XIII. WOUNDS AND DEATH

> *The body keeps a ledger. It settles in full.*

When your Health reaches zero you take a **Wound**. Roll 1d10 on the column matching the damage that did it, apply the result, then reset your Health to its Maximum and subtract any damage that carried over.

When you have taken Wounds equal to your Maximum, you make a **Death Save**.

Some attacks deal Wounds directly, bypassing armor and Damage Reduction entirely. Those are the ones to worry about.

**Lethal** means you are dying. **You will make a Death Save in 1d10 rounds** unless somebody stabilizes you first: a successful *Field Medicine* or *Surgery* check, a Working, or in desperate cases a hot iron and a Body Save. Stabilizing does not fix the injury. It only stops the clock.

**Fatal** means the Death Save is now.

## XIII.1 BLEEDING

Some weapons and some Wounds leave you **Bleeding**: you take **1 damage every round** until it is stopped. It is cumulative. Bleeding at 1 per round and then taking Bleeding +1 means 2 per round thereafter. **Bleeding ignores armor and Damage Reduction.** It has killed more people than every monster in this book.

## XIII.2 DEATH

When your death arrives, make it worth something. Save someone. Answer the question. Buy the others the thirty seconds they need.

The Warden puts 1d10 in a cup, shakes it, and sets it face down on the table without looking. It stays there. **It is revealed only when somebody spends a turn checking your body**, and not before.

| 1d10 | |
|---|---|
| **00** | **Unconscious.** You wake in 2d10 minutes. Reduce your Maximum Health by 1d5. |
| **01-04** | **Unconscious and dying.** You die in 1d5 rounds without intervention. |
| **05-09** | **Dead.** Make a new character. |

# XIV. RANGE

Distance is tracked in bands, not in paces. What matters is whether a thing can touch you, reach you, shoot you, or none of these.

| Band | What it means | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjacent** | It can touch you. Fists, knives, first aid, a whisper, a smell. | Under 1m |
| **Close** | It can get to you inside a few seconds. You can throw something and hit. | 5 to 10m |
| **Long** | You can shoot it. It takes a round or more to close. You would have to shout. | 20 to 100m |
| **Extreme** | You can hear them scream and you may not know from where. | Over 100m |

Underground, Extreme Range mostly stops existing and Long Range becomes rare, which is exactly why the deep places are so much worse than the field.

# XV. SURVIVAL

> *The world was not built for you. It was here first, and it will be here after.*

## XV.1 DARKNESS

Without light you are at **Disadvantage on everything**, you move at a crawl, and you cannot map or keep your bearings.

More importantly, a great deal simply becomes **impossible rather than merely hard**. You cannot read. You cannot do fine work. You cannot track, aim at anything beyond Adjacent, tell one door from another, identify what you are looking at, notice anything that is not making noise, or find something you have dropped. The Warden decides which attempts fall on which side, and the answer is usually that you cannot.

Carry a lantern. Carry two.

## XV.2 AIRS

**Foul air** is unbreathable but not corrosive: sumps, sealed cellars, the bottoms of old wells, anywhere the world has stopped moving. Without a veil, a sponge, or armor with its own supply, make a **Body Save each round.** On a failure you are retching, streaming at the eyes, and fighting for breath: **[-] on all rolls** until you get clean air and a minute to recover. This does not stack; it does not need to.

After **ten minutes** of continuous exposure you begin taking **1d10 damage per round** regardless of any Save. Nobody lasts that long on purpose.

A caged bird gives you about a minute of warning, which is a minute more than nothing.

**Caustic fume** actively destroys: vents, spills, and certain Blighted ground. **1 damage per round** at its mildest, **10 per round** at its worst. Anything beyond that cannot be crossed at all without equipment nobody in your company owns.

## XV.3 WAYFARING SICKNESS

Long passage by sea, caravan, or forced march delivers you to the job already ruined: shaking, sun-blind, saddle-wrecked, and slow. **[-] on all rolls for one week.**

A vigor draught cures it at once. A bed, a bath, and a week of nothing will also do it, if the work will wait.

## XV.4 EXHAUSTION

After twelve hours of hard activity, make a **Body Save every hour.** On a failure, gain 1 Stress and take 1 damage. After twenty-four hours, **all rolls at Disadvantage** until you get eight hours of proper rest.

## XV.5 FOOD AND WATER

A person lives roughly three weeks without food, but **after twenty-four hours without it you are at Disadvantage on everything.**

Water is the real number. **One liter a day is the bare minimum**, and at that level any strenuous act, running, fighting, hauling, forces a **Body Save or you drop.** While water is scarce enough that you are counting it, you are at **Disadvantage on all rolls.**

## XV.6 TEMPERATURE

In extreme cold or heat, make a **Body Save every hour.**

**Cold.** Below freezing, hypothermia and frostbite take hold in ten to thirty minutes for anyone dressed wrong, and kill in half an hour to six hours. You must get warm. There is no other treatment.

**Heat.** Over 40C, heat stroke sets in and kills within hours. There is shade, water, and stillness, and there is nothing else.

## XV.7 ROT

There is no medicine in this world worth the name, and more people die of the week after the fight than die in it.

A Wound that is not cleaned and properly dressed within a day: **Body Save, or it festers.**

A festered Wound imposes **[-] on all rolls**. Each day after that, make another **Body Save**. On a failure the rot goes deeper: take **1d10 damage** and reduce your **Maximum Health by 1**, permanently.

On the **third failure**, the limb comes off within the day or you are dead within 1d5. If the Wound is not on a limb, you are simply dead within 1d5.

*Field Medicine*, *Surgery*, or a week in a hospitaller's ward stops the rot where it stands. None of them give back what it has already taken.

## XV.8 OVERDOSE

Take more than one vigor draught in a day and roll 1d10. **If you roll under the number of doses you have taken in the last twenty-four hours, make a Death Save.**

# XVI. THE BLIGHT

> *It does not tempt you. It does not need to. It simply waits. It is generous, but the generosity is the trap.*

Whatever ended the age of wonders left a residue, and the residue is still working. It has no will and wants nothing. It is in the ground under a barrow, in the black water of a drowned temple, in the seam of a mine that should have been sealed, and in every object made back when people still knew how to make such things.

Standing near it puts it into you. It does not come back out.

## XVI.1 YOUR BLIGHT LEVEL

Every character has a **Blight Level**, from 0 to 10. Most begin at 0. The Blighted begin at 3.

**It only goes up.**

**Every level of Blight is a permanent -1 to all Stats and all Saves.** Every one. Strength, Speed, Intellect, Combat, Sanity, Fear, Body. It does not heal.

Beyond that, the level itself has thresholds:

| Level | | |
|---|---|---|
| **1 to 3** | **TOUCHED.** | The marks begin. You start noticing that you understand things nobody taught you. |
| **4 to 6** | **STEEPED.** | Minimum Stress +1. Each dawn, make a **Body Save** or take 1d10 damage. Strangers cross the road. |
| **7 to 9** | **CONSUMED.** | Minimum Stress +1 again. **[-] on all Body Saves.** Rest no longer restores your Health. You are past mending. |
| **10** | **UNDONE.** | **You die in 1d5 days.** Nothing prevents it and nothing delays it. Spend the days well. |

Every time your Blight Level rises, roll 1d10 on the **Marks** table and describe the change aloud. The others are watching it happen to you.

## XVI.2 MARKS OF THE BLIGHT (1d10)

| | |
|---|---|
| 00 | Your hair comes out in the hand. It will not come back. |
| 01 | Your eyes lose their whites. |
| 02 | Your veins stand black beneath the skin, and they branch wrong. |
| 03 | Your teeth loosen. You keep them in a pouch, because throwing them away felt worse. |
| 04 | You cast a shadow in rooms with no light in them. |
| 05 | Your blood will not clot, and it does not run out either. |
| 06 | Animals go silent when you enter. All of them. Every time. |
| 07 | You no longer breathe unless you remember to. |
| 08 | Something behind your ribs shifts when you are perfectly still. |
| 09 | Your reflection is a half-second late, and it has noticed you noticing. |

## XVI.3 GAINING BLIGHT

Ground is rated by how fast it works on you. At the stated interval, make a **Body Save at Disadvantage**. **On a failure, gain 1 Blight.**

| Ground | Where | Save |
|---|---|---|
| **Tainted** | Old battlefields, plague pits, unhallowed ground, a village that was left. | Once per **day** |
| **Steeped** | A sorcerer's floor, an unburied thing, a broken seal, a room where it was done. | Once per **hour** |
| **Quick** | Direct handling of a relic. Standing inside the circle. The thing itself. | Once per **round** |

**Warded gear removes the Disadvantage.** It does not remove the Save. Nothing removes the Save. A warded cloak and veil is the difference between a bad afternoon and a short career, and it costs twelve hundred silver, and this is why.

You also gain Blight from failed Workings, and from certain treatments that ought to know better.

**Blight-purge** does not remove Blight. It suppresses one level for 2d10 minutes at a cost of 1d5 damage, which makes you healthier and weaker at the same time. Work out which of those you need before you drink it.

## XVI.4 GETTING RID OF IT

You do not.

**The Scouring** is the only known treatment: a day and a night of it, performed in three places in the world. It removes **1d5 Blight**. You are at **[-] on all rolls for four weeks** afterward, and **one in ten do not survive the table.**

People sell everything they own for it. People sell other people for it.

# XVII. WORKINGS

> *Any fool can open a door. The trick has never been the opening.*

Sorcery is not a discipline, a gift, or an inheritance. It is what happens when there is enough Blight in a person that the world begins taking their suggestions.

**Anyone with a Blight Level of 1 or higher may attempt a Working.** You do not need training. Nobody taught the first person who did it either. Training, meaning the *Thaumaturgy* skill, does not make you more powerful. It makes you likelier to survive being powerful.

## XVII.1 ATTEMPTING A WORKING

Say which Working you are attempting and exactly what you intend by it. Make a **Sanity Save**, adding *Thaumaturgy* if you hold it. A Working is your whole action for the round, and you may attempt only one per round.

**The Working always happens.** The roll decides only what it takes out of you.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Critical Success** | It works. It costs you nothing. This will happen perhaps twice in a career. |
| **Success** | It works. Gain 1 Stress. |
| **Failure** | It works. Gain 1 Stress and **1 Blight**. |
| **Critical Failure** | It works, and something comes through alongside it. Gain **1d5 Blight** and roll on the Panic Table. |

Read that again. Your Sanity Save is reduced by your Blight Level, so the more Blight you carry the more often you fail, and every failure gives you more Blight. There is no plateau. There is a slope, it runs one way, and it ends at ten.

## XVII.2 THE SEVEN WORKINGS

In each of these, **B** is your current Blight Level.

**RUIN.** Long Range. Something you can see comes apart from the inside. **1d10 damage per Blight Level**, rolled on the Gore & Massive column. At Blight 5 or higher it is Anti-Armor. It does not miss.

**THE VEIL.** You see what is hidden. Look through **B** walls, or **B** hours backward into the room you are standing in, or straight through any false shape a thing is wearing. Anyone watching your face while you do it makes a Sanity Save.

**THE LEASH.** One living thing at Close Range makes an **Instinct Save at Disadvantage**, or a Fear Save at Disadvantage if it is a player character, or obeys one spoken command for **B** rounds. It is aware the entire time. It remembers afterward, and so do you.

**THE MENDING.** Touch. Restore **1d10 Health per Blight Level.** At Blight 5 or higher you may instead close one Wound. The flesh comes back, but it comes back wrong: the patient gains 1 Stress, and must make a **Body Save at Disadvantage or gain 1 Blight.** The scar is never the shape of the injury.

**THE QUICKENING.** A dead body within Close Range stands and obeys for **B** hours. Its Combat and Instinct are each **B x 10** and it has 1 Wound. When the hours run out it comes apart where it stands, all at once, and what is left will not go back together.

**THE WARD.** Mark a threshold. For **B** minutes, nothing carrying Blight may cross it. This includes you, and you will forget that it includes you.

**THE CALLING.** You open something, and you do not choose what comes through.

Make the Working as normal. Then the Warden rolls 1d10 in private and does not say what it was.

| 1d10 | What answers |
|---|---|
| 00-01 | Nothing you can see. Something in this room is different now, and you will not learn what for a long time. |
| 02-04 | A dead thing local to this place. It rises, and it is angry with whoever is nearest, which is you. |
| 05-06 | Something that will bargain. It wants a name, a memory, a year of your life, or a finger. It will honor the deal exactly and not one inch further. |
| 07-08 | Something that will help. Once. And then follow you home. |
| 09 | Something enormously larger than the Working that called it. Everyone present makes a Panic Check. |

The Calling has no stated effect, no stated range, and no stated limit. That is not an oversight.

## XVII.3 A WORD ON RESTRAINT

The arithmetic is plain, and every player works it out within an hour of learning it: a higher Blight Level means a stronger Working, and failing a Working raises your Blight Level. The system pays you for losing control.

That is the point. Take it as far as you dare.

You have ten steps. You are being paid for one job.

# XVIII. PHYSICK

> *The surgeon's fee is exactly what you would otherwise have spent on living.*

## XVIII.1 SHORT REST

Once a day, after six or more hours of rest, make a **Body Save**. On a success, **reset your Health to its Maximum.** Wounds are unaffected and always will be.

## XVIII.2 REAL TREATMENT

# XIX. HOLDFASTS

> *Every place that will take your money will also take something else.*

When you need to repair, resupply, sell what you took, and remember why you started, you go to a holdfast. There are five kinds.

- **Outlaw Holds.** Pirate coves, bandit towns, and free ports past the edge of anybody's peace. No charter reaches here, which makes them cheap, honest about what they are, and considerably worse.
- **Waystations.** Border forts, mining camps, monastery gates, hedge-villages. Minimally staffed, minimally supplied, and glad of the company right up until they are not.
- **Free Towns.** Guild towns, ore towns, muster fields, garrisons. They dig the ore, forge the mail, and build the ships that hold the world together, and they do it in ten-hour shifts.
- **Great Cities.** Overcrowded, brokered, and stratified beyond any hope of reform. Everything exists here, and more, if you know who to ask.
- **Seats.** The walled estates and spa-cities of people who own counties. Guarded. Invitation only. You will be admitted through a different door.

These also work for districts within a single city.

## XIX.1 SHORE LEAVE

Between contracts, if you have silver left, you can spend it turning what the work did to you into something you can use. This takes **2d10 days**. Less than that and the Warden may rule it did nothing at all.

The cheap places convert the most Stress and cost you the most in every other way. That is the trade, and everybody makes it at least once.

**1. Pay the cost.**

**2. Make a Sanity Save.**

| Holdfast | Cost | Stress converted | Reckoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outlaw Hold | 2d10 x 10 sp | 2d10 [+] | 6 |
| Waystation | 2d10 x 25 sp | 1d5 | 4 |
| Free Town | 2d10 x 100 sp | 1d10 | 3 |
| Great City | 2d10 x 400 sp | 2d10 | 2 |
| Seat | 2d10 x 2,000 sp | All of it | 1 |

- **Success.** Convert Stress up to the amount shown. All remaining Stress is relieved, setting you back to your Minimum.
- **Critical Success.** Convert the maximum the holdfast allows. Relieve the rest.
- **Failure.** Convert nothing. All Stress is relieved to your Minimum anyway. Then gain 1 Stress for the failed Save.
- **Critical Failure.** Convert nothing, relieve nothing, and make a Panic Check.

**3. Convert.** Each point of Stress converted raises **any one Save by 1**, permanently. Divide them as you like. This is the only way any of you will ever improve quickly, and it is paid for with the exact damage that made it possible.

**4. The Reckoning.** Roll 1d10. If you roll **equal to or under the holdfast's Reckoning number**, roll once on the table below. You did something here, and here is what it cost.

| 1d10 | The Reckoning |
|---|---|

| 03 | Something followed you back. It has not shown itself yet. |

# XX. RETAINERS

> *Hire well. Pay on time. Bury what is left.*

At any holdfast you can find people broke enough to come with you. Be careful. Most of them have a reason to be broke.

Retainers are simpler than you. They have four numbers:

- **Combat.** As yours.
- **Instinct.** A single number standing in for Fear, Sanity, Body, Speed, and Intellect together.
- **Maximum Wounds.** They do not track Health. **Any damage they take is one Wound.** At their maximum, they die.
- **Loyalty.** A Save, rolled **2d10+10** after hiring, and rolled again whenever they must choose between your interest and their own. Succeed and they help you. Fail and they help themselves.

**Motivation.** Not every retainer needs one, only the memorable ones. A retainer with a Motivation **always fails a Loyalty Save** when the two are in conflict. Always.

**Wages** are paid monthly, in advance. Short them or stiff them and every future Loyalty Save is at **[-]**.

**Danger money.** A retainer expects an extra month's wage, sometimes two, before they will walk into the ground you are asking them to walk into. Negotiate it at the fire, not at the mouth of the tunnel.

**The death-price.** Every retainer names a sum and a beneficiary before setting out, usually a year's wage, going to a spouse, a parish, or their guild. Guilded retainers have it written down and their guild collects whether you like it or not. The unguilded have only your word. Pay it anyway. Word travels faster than you do, and the next holdfast will have heard.

**Loyalty grows.** A retainer who survives a job and is paid in full gains +1 Loyalty. Larger gains are for saving their life or splitting a real payday with them.

**Who plays them.** Whoever hired them, or whoever has no character in the scene. Keep everyone at the table doing something.

**Replacements.** When your character dies, a retainer is the natural successor. Roll their Stats normally, drop the Instinct number, re-roll Combat, and give them Skills. They will be worse than they were as a retainer, and better than they were as a person.

# THE WHOLE GAME, IN ONE PAGE

**STAT CHECK.** Roll 1d100 under Strength, Speed, Intellect, or Combat. Fail and gain 1 Stress. 90 to 99 always fails.

**SAVE.** Roll 1d100 under Sanity, Fear, or Body. Fail and gain 1 Stress. 90 to 99 always fails.

**WHEN TO ROLL.** Only when failure costs something. Otherwise you succeed.

**SKILLS.** +10 Trained, +15 Expert, +20 Master. Without the Skill you usually still roll, at no bonus and often at [-]. Reading, unknown tongues, Master subjects, unfamiliar mechanisms, and formal medicine cannot be attempted at all.

**[+] / [-].** Roll twice, take the better or the worse. They cancel.

**CRITICALS.** Doubles. Good on a success. On a failure, something bad happens and you make a Panic Check.

**PANIC.** Roll 1d20 over your current Stress or consult the Panic Table.

**DAMAGE.** Subtract from Health. At zero, take a Wound, roll on the Wounds table, reset Health to Maximum minus carryover. At Maximum Wounds, Death Save.

**ARMOR.** Ignore damage below your total AP. Damage at or above your AP destroys the armor and carries through. AA ignores and destroys armor. DR always applies first.

**ARMOR TYPES.** Crushing [+] against Plate, [-] against Soft. Piercing [+] against Mail, [-] against Plate. Cutting [+] against Soft, [-] against Mail. Fire ignores all of it.

**RANGE.** Adjacent, it can touch you. Close, it can reach you. Long, you can shoot it. Extreme, you can hear it.

**BLIGHT.** Permanent, and it only rises. Every level is -1 to every Stat and Save. Body Save at [-] to resist gaining it; warded gear removes the [-] and nothing removes the Save. Steeped at 4. Consumed at 7. Dead at 10.

**WORKINGS.** Sanity Save. It always works. Failure costs 1 Blight. Critical failure costs 1d5 and a Panic Check. Power scales with Blight.
